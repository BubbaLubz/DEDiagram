# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, equally central. **Solo**: an individual data engineer or architect prototyping a pipeline alone, often starting from a vague idea and using AI generation to get to a plausible first draft fast. **Team**: a small team or club drafting pipeline architecture together, live, on the same diagram at the same time — the reason the app has real-time multi-user editing at all. The same canvas has to feel complete for one person and extend cleanly into a shared session without changing how it behaves.

## Product Purpose

DEDiagram takes someone from a plain-language description of a data pipeline (or a blank canvas) to a real, positioned architecture diagram, then lets them keep working on it — alone or with teammates editing live at the same time. Success looks different depending on who's in the room: a team leaves a session with a diagram everyone actually agrees represents the pipeline, replacing whiteboards and slide decks for that purpose; a solo session produces a solid draft that's ready to bring into exactly that kind of team review.

## Positioning

Neither AI generation nor live collaboration alone is the differentiator — the combination is. Describe a pipeline in plain language, get a real diagram back, and then work on it together with a team in real time, in the same tool.

## Brand Personality

Precise and technical, not corporate — now expressed through a specific material language rather than generic dev-tool chrome: warm wood tones, matte plaster, quiet grain texture, drawn directly from contemporary Japanese minimalist architecture. Calm, unhurried, deliberate about where color and texture appear. Built by engineers for engineers, but the craft shows in restraint and material honesty rather than density for its own sake.

## Anti-references

Generic enterprise SaaS: bland blue-and-white dashboards, stock icon grids, generic stat tiles, corporate stock-photography energy. If a screen could be mistaken for a generic B2B dashboard template, it's failed. Also explicitly rejected: gradient CTAs and gradient text (the canonical "AI-generated design" tell), glassmorphism, and any cool blue-black "dev tool dark mode" default — the palette is warm and material, drawn from two reference photographs of Japanese minimalist interiors, not from a generic dark-mode template.

## Design Principles

- **Show, don't gate.** The diagram and its notes are the primary surface — chrome (toolbars, modals, panels) should recede so the pipeline itself stays legible.
- **Precision over decoration.** Every visual choice should read as intentional and technical, never as decorative filler. This is a tool for engineers, not a marketing surface.
- **Solo-first, team-ready.** Every interaction has to work completely for one person working alone, and extend cleanly into a live multi-user session without feeling like a different mode.
- **Earn trust before asking for it.** AI-generated output — diagrams, clarifying questions — should show its reasoning or assumptions rather than presenting a black-box result.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal compliance target — this is an internal/team tool, not a public product with compliance obligations. Still expected as a baseline: solid contrast throughout, full keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion support wherever animation is used.
